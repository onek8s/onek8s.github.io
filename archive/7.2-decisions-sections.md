(a) was how platforms of the 2010s grew their service meshes and operators nobody asked for | Decided (user, 2026-10-17) |
| D65 | Dapr as an application building block (state, pub/sub, bindings, service invocation, workflows, actors) | (a) **Not offered**: applications talk to PaaS services over their open protocols ([section 1.3](../design/1.3-open-standards-and-portability.md#data-in-paas-services-that-every-cloud-offers)); (b) the AKS Dapr extension in every cell as part of the application contract; (c) the extension per cell on request, components and identities generated by the platform | **(a)**, with (c) as an option kept open ([section 7.4](7.4-outlook-2030-and-the-options-kept-open.md#the-options-kept-open)) | Against the five tests of D64: nothing names it; the cloud manages only the control plane (runtime fixes go to the open-source project, a new version re-creates the extension); an application written to the Dapr API needs it in every cell it runs in, so it becomes fleet-wide with its first user; applications notice - the Dapr API becomes their contract; the way out is rewriting them to the broker's protocol. Cost: a daprd sidecar per pod (100m CPU and 250 MiB requested by Dapr's guidance), a control plane on the system pool (about 0.85 CPU without HA, three times that with HA), and placement and the scheduler want persistent volumes a stateless cell does not have. Sec: sentry's mTLS between sidecars is the mesh R9b and D38 leave out, with a second CA. For it: CNCF graduated, durable workflows and actors that the PaaS protocols do not give | Proposed (user's question, 2026-10-17) |
